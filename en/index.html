The summit

India’s sustainability conversation has mostly happened in English, in a handful of cities, among a handful of large companies. The real work — a village reviving its water bodies, an MSME cutting its power bill through better design, a farmer collective rebuilding its soil — is happening everywhere else, often undocumented and almost never in the room where policy gets made.

NBT Vasudha is built to close that gap: one stage, one standard, two tracks — Bharat and corporate India — talking to each other instead of past each other.

Why a summit built this way

Designed for the businesses and people who make up most of the country

Most sustainability platforms in India are priced, written and judged for a large-corporate marketing budget. NBT Vasudha is designed from the ground up to also work for everyone else.

Bilingual by design, not by translation

Every session runs in Hindi and English — this is not an English event with a Hindi banner.

One stage, 48 award categories

Recognition across 8 responsible development pillars — from clean energy to inclusive action — honoured at the same evening ceremony.

Five published evaluation criteria, before you nominate

You can read exactly how entries are judged on the Awards page, before you nominate.

Open to the public, not just the invited

Delegate passes are ticketed and on sale — this is a summit you can walk into, not one you need an introduction for.

Built on Navbharat Times’ own reach

Into Delhi–NCR, Uttar Pradesh and the wider Hindi-speaking readership that most sustainability platforms never reach at all.

The programme

Five conversations, one day

Nation-building & net zero

India’s 2070 target, read from outside the metros — what it actually asks of districts, not just boardrooms.

Natural capital & finance

Making sustainability bankable for businesses too small to interest an ESG fund.

Village to global

How a local fix — in water, waste or farming — becomes a national template.

Policy & compliance, sized right

What disclosure and regulation actually mean for a business with fifty employees, not fifty thousand.

The language bridge

Why climate information that only exists in English reaches a fraction of the people who need to act on it.

Summit — questions

Questions

Do I need an invitation to attend?

No. Delegate passes are sold openly. A small number of seats are reserved for government and jury guests, but the summit floor is ticketed and public.

Which language are the sessions in?

Both. Every track runs with live Hindi–English interpretation.

Is there a discount for students and NGOs?

Yes — that is exactly what the Bharat Pass is priced for. Valid student ID or NGO registration is required at check-in.

Can I upgrade my pass later?

Yes, you can upgrade from a Professional to a Vasudha Leadership Pass any time before 1 February 2027, by paying the difference.

How is this connected to the NBT Vasudha Awards?

The Awards are the evening component of the same day. Nominations, categories and the judging method are on the Awards page.
